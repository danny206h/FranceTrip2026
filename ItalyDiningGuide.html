Selection philosophy

The Restaurant Filter

The list favors food that belongs to its place: freshwater fish and polenta on Como, Tuscan grills and humble soups in Florence, farm cooking in the countryside, and anchovies, pesto and seafood in Liguria. A beautiful setting counts—but never excuses mediocre food.

Lombardy • lake and mountain

Lake Como & Bellagio

Look beyond generic pasta toward perch, lavarello, missoltini, risotto, polenta uncia and Alpine cheeses. San Giovanni and the hills above Bellagio usually feel more rooted than the busiest waterfront.

Wine to drink here

  • Terre Lariane IGT white with lake fish—the most local choice.
  • Rosso di Valtellina for an easy lunch red.
  • Valtellina Superiore DOCG (Nebbiolo/Chiavennasca) with polenta or meat; look for Sassella, Grumello, Inferno or Valgella.
  • Sforzato di Valtellina only for a rich special meal.
Ask: “Do you have a local wine from Lake Como or Valtellina?”

Tuscany • city cooking

Florence

Florence is strongest when the cooking is direct: bistecca, ribollita, pappa al pomodoro, tripe, roast meats, beans and excellent olive oil. Avoid ordering bistecca for one—it is priced by weight and intended to share.

Wine to drink here

  • Chianti Classico is the default: Sangiovese with pasta, ragù and grilled meat.
  • Chianti Classico Riserva for bistecca or a longer dinner.
  • Carmignano DOCG is a smart, less-obvious local red west of Florence.
  • Vernaccia di San Gimignano for white; Vin Santo with cantucci.
Do not pay Super-Tuscan prices simply because the label is famous; ask for a good local Sangiovese.

Tuscany • agriturismo week

The Countryside

The exact agriturismo location will determine what is sensible. Use this as a regional selector, not a reason to drive two hours for dinner. The best rule is to eat at the farm whenever it cooks, then choose one destination meal in the nearest wine zone.

Choose wine by where you are

  • Chianti Classico: between Florence and Siena; versatile Sangiovese.
  • Brunello di Montalcino: structured, costly, best with meat; Rosso di Montalcino is the easier choice.
  • Vino Nobile di Montepulciano: polished Sangiovese, often better value than Brunello.
  • Bolgheri: Cabernet/Merlot-led blends on the coast—excellent but rarely bargains.
At the agriturismo, start with its own wine and olive oil. That is the point of staying there.

Liguria • sea and terraces

Cinque Terre

The essentials are Monterosso anchovies, pesto, focaccia, stuffed mussels, seafood pasta and the region’s herb-and-olive-oil brightness. The villages are busy; reserve dinners and use lunch for focaccia or anchovies rather than chasing another full restaurant.

Wine to drink here

  • Cinque Terre DOC white is the default: dry, saline and ideal with seafood.
  • Sciacchetrà DOC is the rare sweet passito wine—order a glass with dessert or cheese, not as the table wine.
  • Vermentino Colli di Luni is the useful wider-Liguria fallback.
If the restaurant offers a local producer by the glass, take it—the terraced vineyards are tiny and bottles rarely travel far.
